The 2026–27 Japan Rugby League One – Division 1 (Japanese: NTTジャパンラグビー リーグワン2026–27), known as the NTT 2026–27 Japan Rugby League One for sponsorship reasons, is the sixth season of the Japan Rugby League One (JRLO) and the 23rd season in top-flight Japanese rugby. The season schedule was released in early July 2026, with the season scheduled to begin on 12 December, with the season to conclude in a play-off final on 6 June 2027.[1] The competition consists of twelve teams. The team composition remained unchanged from the previous season with the two teams in the relegation zone (Sagamihara DynaBoars,[2] Urayasu D-Rocks)[3] defeating their Division 2 opponents in the relegation play-offs.

The Kobe Steelers are the defending champions, having defeated the Kubota Spears in the final 22–13 at the Japan National Stadium in Tokyo.[4][5] It was their third title in top-flight Japanese rugby, and their first since the inception of the JRLO in 2022.[6]

Background

[edit]

Format

[edit]

Unlike the previous seasons of the Japan Rugby League One – Division 1, the 2026–27 had removed the conference system format,[1] which had been in place since the inaugural 2022 season.[7]

On 9 September 2026, the Japan Rugby League One released the fixture list for all three divisions. Division 1 set each team to face 11 different opponents, playing seven of them twice and the remaining four once, to give a total of 18 matches per team across the league.[8] The top six placed teams on the league ladder at the end of these matches would then advance to the play-offs to challenge for the Japanese title, with the top two having a bye round into the semi-finals.[8] The teams whom finished 11th and 12th, respectively, would advance into a relegation play-off against the top-two placed teams in Division 2 for a place in the top division.[8]

Team name and location changes

[edit]

Ahead of the season, two clubs changed their names with one additionally changing locations. The first team to formally change their name was the Tochigi Honda Heat (Japanese: 栃木ホンダヒート), formerly known as the Mie Honda Heat (Japanese: 三重ホンダヒート).[9][10] Initially announced in 2024, the team's general manager stated in an interview that the location move and name change was largely motivated by the league's policy of commercial viability. With Suzuka being difficult in attracting spectators and having a smaller population size, the teams decision was to relocate closer to Tokyo (where most of the Division 1 clubs are located).[11]

In late September 2026, the Toshiba Brave Lupus (Japanese: 東芝ブレイブルーパス東京) followed suit and changed their club name to the Toshiba Brave Lupus Musashi (Japanese: 東芝ブレイブルーパス武蔵), with the formal change taking place on 1 October 2026.[12] The change was made in an effort to tie the club closer to the historic Japanese province of Musashi, a region which was first established during the Asuka period.[12]

International players

[edit]
See also: 2026–27 Japan Rugby League One foreign player policy change

In the Japan Rugby League One, Category C players are internationally Test-capped players who are not eligible to represent Japan. There are three that are allowed in each teams matchday squad.[22][23]

In September 2026, the Toshiba Brave Lupus announced the signing of a fourth Category C player. However, this signing was as an injury cover player, and he would not be eligible to play unless this circumstance were to occur.[24]
